The Transcendent Mandate

Apostle Benjamin Zulu President & Founder 12 min read

What is hope? This is a question that has intrigued mankind for millennia. Some think hope comes from sound government policy, whereas others think hope comes from having a lot of money in their bank accounts, or from having a wonderful spouse. However, experience has shown otherwise.

There are billionaires today who have no hope. There are celebrities committing suicide, not to mention the alarming rate of divorce throughout the world. That tells us one thing: the world is still searching for hope. The United Nations was created as a vehicle through which peace could be driven throughout the globe, yet its actions have consistently fallen short of its objectives.

Where is the problem, then? Could it be that hope and peace are products of the Transcendent? Let us explore.

Isaiah 9:6–7 · John 14What is hope?

Incidentally, the Word of God calls Jesus Christ the Prince of Peace. This means that at the core of the salvation package is peace, which brings to light the truth that without Jesus there is no peace. In John 14, Jesus speaks of offering the kind of peace that this world cannot give and is incapable of offering. Therefore, no matter how hard the world tries, it will never be able to offer that kind of peace.

Wait a minute — are we not dealing with hope? Then why have we started talking about peace? Relax, buckle up and lean back, because we are about to embark on some startling revelations.

There is no hope without peace, because peace is what gives you the ability to hope. Imagine this scenario. You have been told that a particular herb can cure stomach pains. After using that herb the pain multiplies and your health begins to dissipate. Then the seller comes to you again, telling you there is another herb that can cure the worsening situation. Would you believe him? Of course not. Why? Because the first herb did not deliver what he promised.

You might even call his herbs hopeless — because they rob you of the hope of being cured. And what does that mean in practice? It means they make you uncomfortable, and that discomfort is the result of peace being robbed from your spirit. There is an inextricable link between peace and hope.

Now flip the example over. Imagine the first herb had eliminated the stomach pains on the very first day. You would be at peace, and very hopeful of being healed, because the herb has given you a glimpse of a bright future. It has assured you that you will soon be well. That assurance that all will be well is what we call hope.

1 Corinthians 13:13 · 1 Timothy 1:1Hope is a Person

Thus far we have noted that hope is the assurance of a better tomorrow. Going by human standards, who can guarantee that tomorrow? Who holds the cure to the atrocities being committed, or to the calamities plaguing the world left, right and centre?

In this technological age it is very easy to lose hope. All you need to do is pick up a remote, switch on the television and watch the news. There are rumours of wars everywhere you turn. The name of Jesus has become an offence on many university and college campuses around the world. Atheism is quickly taking over the world’s intelligentsia. We can see that hope is so gigantic that no human being is big enough to offer it.

The Bible, however, holds some shocking truths about hope. 1 Corinthians 13:13 states that “now abideth faith, hope, charity, these three; but the greatest of these is charity.” Hope is everlasting — it never dies. And Romans 5:5 tells us that “hope maketh not ashamed, because the love of God is shed abroad in our hearts by the Holy Ghost which is given unto us.” In other words, the person who hopes in the Lord will never be ashamed.

Paul, an apostle of Jesus Christ by the commandment of God our Saviour, and Lord Jesus Christ, which is our hope.

1 Timothy 1:1

This lets us know that Jesus Christ is our hope. Jesus Christ is also the Word of God; therefore our hope is anchored on the Word of God. No matter what we see happening in the world, we know that the Word of God is our guide and our hope. Paul summed the revelation up in Colossians 1:27: “Christ in you, the hope of glory.” That glory was lost when Adam sinned, but Christ — the second Adam — came to restore it. Glory to God!

It is crystal clear from these scriptures that hope is found only in Jesus Christ, and so I dare say that the person who does not have Jesus Christ in their heart is hopeless. Ephesians 2:11–12 sheds more light on this, reminding the Gentiles that at that time they were “without Christ… having no hope, and without God in the world.” That should give you an idea of the true state of affairs in the world as we speak.

Hope is not a feeling you generate. It is a Person you are joined to.

Matthew 28:18–20 · 2 Corinthians 5:17Where do we come in?

The world is in desperate need of hope. When children in some schools in America and Canada were asked what their greatest need was, the dominant answer was a saviour. Others said they needed Batman, Spiderman or Hercules — impliedly saying, “we need a super hero.” Guess what? That super hero is Jesus Christ.

Therefore our role is to preach Jesus throughout the world. Matthew 28:18–20 gives the great commission, which is compulsory for every Christian. However, you cannot give what you do not have. If you are hopeless yourself, how can you minister hope to others?

The good news is that Jesus has made us like Himself. We carry the nature of God, and we have become God’s extended hand to the world. 2 Corinthians 5:17 says, “Therefore if any man be in Christ, he is a new creature: old things are passed away; behold, all things are become new.” The Amplified translation puts it brilliantly: “if any person is ingrafted in Christ, he is a new creation… the old previous moral and spiritual condition has passed away. Behold, the fresh and new has come.”

When you are born again, you are literally born anew. It means you have no past. It means you look like Jesus Christ, because you are born of God. As 1 John 4:17 puts it — “because as He is, so are we in this world.” There you have it. We are the kind of Christ-ones the world is waiting for. Obadiah 1:21 speaks of saviours coming up on Mount Zion; those saviours are the believers in the body of Christ.

Isaiah, foreseeing the coming Messiah, wrote that “he shall see his seed, he shall prolong his days, and the pleasure of the LORD shall prosper in his hand” (Isaiah 53:10). God has prolonged the days of Jesus Christ through believers who are doing the very things Jesus would be doing here on earth. As long as we continue to do the will of God, the Almighty does not need to send Jesus again to die for the world. Jesus is the power working in us and for us as we reach out to the lost.

John 3:16 · 1 John 5:13What is the transcendent life?

John 3:16 says, “For God so loved the world that He gave His only begotten Son, that whosoever believeth in Him should not perish, but have everlasting life.” Once you have been born again you have the life of God in your spirit — eternal life. Many people have a limited interpretation of that term. Ask the average Christian what eternal life means and the answer is, “a life which is endless.” I could not agree more — only that the definition is limited in scope.

Eternal life is the life of God. It is the life that makes God, God — the life responsible for giving God the qualities of God. Can God get sick? Can He get confused? The questions already sound absurd, because being God presupposes omnipotence. The answers are obvious: He cannot. And the reason He cannot is that the qualities of that life make it impossible for Him to perform otherwise.

What is the significance of this? God has given us that very life. People say, “well, in heaven we will have eternal life, but on earth there is no sweet without sweat.” I am glad that statement does not apply to everyone who is born again. Eternal life, contrary to popular opinion, is a present-hour reality.

I write this to you who believe in the name of the Son of God, so that you may know, with settled and absolute knowledge, that you already have life, yes, eternal life.

1 John 5:13, Amplified

Clear as day: we are not trying to earn eternal life, because we already have it. And if that is true, then I am expected to manifest the qualities of God. Eternal life is a life above all the natural elements of this world. It supersedes and supplants the human life. It is therefore the transcendent life.

When you are born again, you are born with the transcendent life at work in your spirit. When you take advantage of that life, you will transcend the circumstances of life. Only God can place a limit on what you can do. Psalm 82:6 says, “I have said, Ye are gods; and all of you are children of the Most High.” Simple logic: a dog gives birth to a puppy, and God gives birth to a god. We are in the class of gods. 2 Peter 1:4 says we are partakers of the divine nature. That is so big — it means we are of the God kind. When we refuse to be ordinary we are not being stiff-necked; it is because we know who we are.

1 Peter 2:9 · John 10:10What does ‘transcendent’ mean?

The word transcendent derives from transcend, a transliteration of the Latin transcendere — to go over, as water goes over a vessel because it is full. Among its synonyms are supernatural, superior, paranormal, excellent and magnificent. It also means free from the constraints of the material world. Thus we are a species of being free from the constraints of the material world, except those placed on us by God.

But you are a chosen race, a royal priesthood, a dedicated nation, God’s own purchased, special people, that you may set forth the wonderful deeds and display the virtues and perfections of Him who called you out of darkness into His marvellous light.

1 Peter 2:9, Amplified

We are not ordinary. We are a special breed of people called to show forth the excellences and virtues of God. So when you meet a scoffer who tells you, “the problem is that you think you are special,” look them eyeball to eyeball and say, “the problem is that you are ignorant of Scripture — I am actually special.”

God wants to demonstrate His power through us, and the world has not seen anything yet. They will witness the kind of wisdom that sends shockwaves across the intelligentsia. They will experience a level of miracles that leaves them bemused. We are more glorious than the saints of the Old Testament, so for us the supernatural has become daily bread.

John 10:10 in the Amplified Version reads: “The thief comes only in order to steal and kill and destroy. I came that they may have and enjoy life, and have it in abundance — to the full, till it overflows.” That overflowing life is what we have. So if you are not enjoying life, then you are not living the transcendent life; you are living below your potential.

Colossians 2:15 · Hebrews 2:14Where is the devil now?

Previously the devil had power over mankind by virtue of the transgression of Adam. When Adam sinned he handed the dominion God had given him to the devil, and the devil could torment anyone on earth freely. The position today is different. Jesus defeated the devil and took the keys of death and hell from him.

God disarmed the principalities and powers that were ranged against us, and made a bold display and public example of them, in triumphing over them in Him and in it, by the cross.

Colossians 2:15, Amplified

The principalities and powers have been disarmed. Your victory is therefore guaranteed. You do not need to fight the devil; you only need to command him when you want him to go, because he is paralysed. Hebrews 2:14 says that through death Christ brought to nought and made of no effect him who had the power of death, that is, the devil. Look carefully at the tense. Had. Past tense. The devil no longer has the power of death, because he has been brought to zero.

This begs the question: who has the power of death, if not the devil? We will let the Bible answer. Revelation 1:18 — “I am he that liveth, and was dead; and, behold, I am alive for evermore, and have the keys of hell and of death.” How can you fear death or the devil when Jesus is the One holding the key?

What kills Christians, then? One word: ignorance. Hosea 4:6 — “My people are destroyed for lack of knowledge.” Once you know the truth and apply it, you become transcendent. So when you find yourself fighting the devil or demons, know that you are in the wrong fight. What is the right fight for a Christian? The fight of faith. 1 Timothy 6:12 — “Fight the good fight of faith, lay hold on eternal life, whereunto thou art also called.”

Don’t fight the devil. Fight ignorance. That is what he is using to destroy believers.

Fill yourself up with the Word, and transcend. Welcome to the year of the supernatural. Go and prosper.
